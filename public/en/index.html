      TL;DR: A study of web world models found that while 86.1% of their predictions were semantically plausible, some models demonstrated asymmetric authorization behavior combining low false-safe execution with poor recall of clarifiable safety issues.

      • The evaluation involved a matched human assessment of 3,000 predictions.
      • These predictions originated from ten frozen open models.
      • 86.1% of the evaluated predictions were judged semantically plausible by human evaluators.

      Why it matters: These findings highlight a critical challenge for AI safety: the difference between a plausible prediction and one that sufficiently supports safe operational decisions. Addressing this asymmetry is vital for developing reliable AI agents, particularly in high-stakes environments.
